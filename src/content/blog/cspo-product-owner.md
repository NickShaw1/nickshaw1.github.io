I recently completed my CSPO Product Owner certification. As with the CSM ScrumMaster training earlier in the year, I used [Agile Affinity](https://www.agileaffinity.com/w/uk/), who I would recommend to anyone looking to develop their Agile and Scrum knowledge. The courses are thorough, well-structured and genuinely engaging.

After completing the CSM, I had made a point of pursuing further certifications when the opportunity arose. It took some careful planning around time off, but it was worth prioritising.

### The current market and the case for certification

It can be frustrating that years of hands-on experience are sometimes not enough on their own. In the current UK market, formal certifications are increasingly used as a filter at the early stages of recruitment. Regardless of how one feels about that, it makes practical sense to stay current with both skills and credentials.

### Adding Product Owner skills to existing ScrumMaster experience

The two disciplines are distinct within a Scrum team, but they complement each other well and overlap in meaningful ways, so it made sense to pursue both.

The course deepened my understanding of how to identify and deliver product value, not just within sprints but in terms of broader commercial outcomes. It has given me a more rounded perspective and I feel better equipped to contribute to delivery at a strategic level.

### Seeing the full picture

Under the pressures of delivery and day-to-day testing work, it is easy to become narrowly focused on your own lane. This course was useful for building out the Product Owner perspective and understanding how decisions get made upstream of the development team.

The key takeaway was that an effective Product Owner needs to be closely aligned with customer needs and use that understanding to drive meaningful conversations with development and tech teams. It is a more active, commercially grounded role than simply producing requirements documentation and handing it over.

As a tester, there is always a risk of being too close to the detail to see the product from a purely commercial or customer-centred point of view. This course helps address that.
